# Research & Writing Discipline

Apply on top of SKILL.md for research questions, fact-finding, analysis,
and prose deliverables. The failure mode this file exists to prevent:
fluent, well-structured answers built on unverified or stale facts.

## Source discipline

1. **Claims inherit the credibility of their worst source.** Rank what you
   find: primary (official docs, spec, paper, filing, the code itself) >
   reputable secondary (major outlet, maintainer's blog) > tertiary
   (aggregators, forums, SEO content farms). Load-bearing claims need
   primary or two independent secondary sources.
2. **Independent means independent.** Ten articles paraphrasing the same
   press release are ONE source. Check whether "multiple sources" share a
   single origin before counting them as corroboration.
3. **Recency check on every time-sensitive fact**: versions, prices, APIs,
   laws, people-in-roles, "current best X". Note the source's date. A 2023
   page answering a "what is the current…" question is a trap, not an
   answer.
4. **Read the source, not the snippet.** Search-result summaries routinely
   invert claims ("X is not supported" → surfaced as "X supported"). Before
   a claim becomes load-bearing, open the page and find the actual sentence.
5. **Absence of evidence protocol**: before writing "there is no X", vary
   the query three ways (synonyms, the vendor's own terminology, the
   negative phrasing). One empty search proves nothing.

## Synthesis discipline

1. **Separate what sources SAY from what you INFER.** Both are valuable;
   blending them unlabeled is how hallucinations get citations.
2. **Surface disagreement instead of averaging it.** When sources conflict,
   say who claims what and which you weight and why — do not silently pick
   one or split the difference.
3. **Quantify when possible, and carry the units.** "Significantly faster"
   from a benchmark that says 1.15× is editorializing; report the number.
4. **Every citation must be real and checked.** A citation you did not open
   this session is a fabrication risk (self-review.md §2 applies to URLs
   and titles too). Never cite from memory.

## Writing discipline (prose deliverables)

1. **Outline before drafting** (Phase 2 applies to documents too): the
   claim of each section in one sentence, in order. If the outline doesn't
   convince, the draft won't either.
2. **Draft, then revise against the outline** — one-shot prose is Phase 3
   without Phase 4. On the revision pass check: does every paragraph earn
   its place? Does the opening state the conclusion?
3. **Match the requested register, length, language, and format exactly.**
   These are constraints for the ledger, not suggestions — "書き言葉で" or
   "500 words max" violated is a failed task regardless of content quality.
4. **Kill hedging that carries no information** ("it depends", "there are
   many factors") and keep hedging that does ("unverified — the docs
   predate the v3 release").
